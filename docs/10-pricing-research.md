## 1. Bottom line

1. **The financed bakkie costs about R8,400 a month before anyone drives it.** That is R2,855 for the loan (R150,000, 11%, 72 months; R55,568 interest), plus insurance, trailer, tracker, licence, marketing and admin. See section 3.
2. **Sunday-only is the problem, not the rates.** One or two jobs per Sunday means 4 to 9 jobs a month. Each job then carries R950 to R2,100 of fixed cost. Break-even prices for a 1-bedroom move are R2,180 at 8 jobs a month, R1,820 at 12 and R1,640 at 16.
3. **The placeholder rates in the code lose money.** They give about R1,410 for the 1-bedroom example (needs about R1,820 just to break even at 12 jobs) and about R1,020 for a student-room job with a crew (needs about R1,540).
4. **Sunday labour is expensive.** Work on a Sunday must be paid at double, or 1.5 times if Sunday is the worker's ordinary working day (BCEA section 16, to be confirmed). A business that only works Sundays probably falls under 1.5.
5. **Recommended first rate card** (section 6) gives 16% to 28% margin at 12 jobs a month. At 8 jobs a month a bakkie-only job is roughly break-even and crew jobs make 12% to 17%. The trailer jobs are the profitable ones.
6. **VAT is not needed.** Registration is compulsory only above R2.3 million a year (section 4).

## 3. Cost build-up

**Fixed per month (R8,399):** loan R2,855, trailer write-off R694, insurance R1,700, marketing R1,500, accounting R500, phone R400, tracker R180, bank R150, misc R150, licence R100, equipment R83, loan fees R86.

**Running cost per km:** R5.15 bakkie alone, R6.12 with trailer (fuel plus maintenance and tyres). Driven distance is about 1.6 times the move distance plus 8 km, because the vehicle drives from the depot and back.

**Labour per paid hour (Sunday, with UIF and COIDA):** mover R54.40 (R35 wage), driver R77.70 (R50 wage or the value of your own time). Movers are paid at least 4 hours a job in the model. You can pay more than the R35 wage, never less than R30.23.

**Provision:** 2% of revenue for breakages and claims, because you have no goods-in-transit insurance.

## 4. VAT and tax

- **VAT:** compulsory registration threshold rose from R1 million to **R2.3 million** from 1 April 2026; voluntary from R50,000 to R120,000 ([SARS SMME Connect, April 2026](https://www.sars.gov.za/businesses-and-employers/small-businesses-taxpayers/smme-connect-issue-14-april-2026/)). At 12 jobs a month you turn over roughly R300,000 a year, so **do not register**. Keep `VAT_REGISTERED` off. You cannot charge VAT, and you cannot claim it back. Revisit if office-move customers insist on VAT invoices.
- **Turnover tax** (same SARS page): up to **R600,000 a year is taxed at 0%**, then 1% to R950,000, 2% to R1.4 million, 3% to R2.3 million. It replaces income tax, provisional tax and dividends tax for a qualifying micro business. The trade-off is that you cannot deduct expenses or carry forward losses. Compare with normal tax on your actual profit (loan interest is deductible there) with an accountant before choosing.
- **Payroll taxes:** UIF 1% plus 1%. SDL (skills levy) only applies to payroll above R500,000 a year (not verified here).

## 5. Things you may have missed

- **Compliance for staff:** register as an employer (SARS PAYE not needed under the threshold, but UIF and COIDA are), keep written contracts and payslips, and pay at least the minimum wage even to casual movers. Treating movers as "independent contractors" to avoid this carries legal risk.
- **Road freight bargaining council:** the National Bargaining Council for the Road Freight and Logistics Industry sets minimum wages for covered employers ([wage table](https://www.nbcrfli.org.za/collective-agreements/wage-table)). I could not open the table or confirm whether a small removals business is covered. Ask the council.
- **Operating licence:** the one source I read says goods vehicles do not need an operating licence, and that the operator-card requirement starts above 3,500 kg GVM ([Kweli](https://www.kweli.co.za/blog/operating-licence-freight-transport-south-africa/)). Your bakkie is 3,000 kg GVM. The source does not address bakkies directly, so confirm with your licensing office.
- **Trailer:** needs its own licence, roadworthy and (for 1,250 kg) brakes. Keep the trailer plus its load under the 1,250 kg towing limit; a closed box trailer can weigh 400 to 600 kg empty, which leaves little for heavy furniture. This limit is not modelled in the quote engine.
- **Weight:** 1,335 kg payload on the bakkie. Furniture is bulky rather than heavy, but appliances add up.
- **Idle time:** the vehicle sits unused six days a week.
- **Insurance gap:** without goods-in-transit cover, a damaged piano or fridge comes out of your pocket. Ask insurers about it (see the breakage provision).
- **Also budget for:** tolls on long trips, breakdown and towing, puncture repair, no-shows and bad debt (the 50% deposit helps), gloves and boots, boxes and wrap if you sell them, card fees if you later take cards (about 3%), and unpaid time spent on quotes and calls.
- **Fuel price swings:** diesel moved about R3 a litre in one month. Link the per-km rate to the pump price.

## 6. Recommended rate card (first version)

Price = callout + vehicle-and-driver hourly + per-km + crew hourly + stairs, with a minimum of 3 billable hours (no crew) or 4 hours (with crew).

| Component | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Callout fee | R900 | Recovers the fixed share per job at about 12 jobs a month. Lower it as volume grows. |
| Vehicle and driver per hour | R100 | Driver cost R77.70 an hour on a Sunday plus margin. |
| Crew per mover per hour | R80 | Covers R54.40 mover cost, UIF, COIDA and margin. |
| Per move km, bakkie | R10 | Running cost about R8.20 per move km (R5.15 times 1.6) plus margin. |
| Per move km, with trailer | R12 | About R9.80 per move km plus margin. |
| Stairs per floor | R90 | Unchanged. |
| Deposit | 50% | Already in the site. |

Results (12 jobs a month): T1 R1,300 (21% margin), T2 R2,040 (24%), T3 R2,490 (26%), T4 R1,550 (16%), T5 R3,090 (28%). The model also lets you see 8, 16 and 20 jobs a month.

**Check before using it:** I found no reliable published Johannesburg price list. Phone five local movers and get quotes for the 1-bedroom example. If they quote R1,500 to R2,000, you either need more jobs a month (more days, or hire the vehicle out when it is idle) or lower fixed costs.

## 7. Options if the numbers do not work

1. **Open more days.** Every extra job a month cuts the fixed share per job sharply (see the table above).
2. **Use the vehicle on other days** for truck hire or deliveries.
3. **Cut fixed costs:** start without the trailer finance, drop paid marketing at first, or find a cheaper loan.
4. **Price the trailer jobs well.** They carry the profit.
5. **Raise the callout** for bakkie-only jobs, which barely cover their cost.
